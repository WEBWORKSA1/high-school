💚 Student Wellbeing: Managing Stress, Sleep & Balance

Practical, evidence-based ways for high school students to manage stress, sleep better, handle test anxiety and know when and where to get support.

📅 Updated October 2026✍️ High.School Editorial Team✅ Editorial policy

Some stress is normal — it can even help you prepare. But chronic stress, poor sleep and constant pressure make learning harder. These habits help, and asking for support is a sign of strength.

Sleep is a study tool

Teens are recommended to get 8–10 hours of sleep. Keep a consistent schedule, charge your phone outside the bedroom, and avoid caffeine late in the day.

Managing test anxiety

  • Prepare with practice tests under realistic conditions — familiarity reduces fear.
  • Use slow breathing before and during exams (inhale 4 counts, exhale 6).
  • Reframe nerves as energy: “I’m ready” rather than “I’m scared.”
  • Start with questions you know to build momentum.

Daily balance

Movement, time outdoors, time with friends and offline hobbies all buffer stress. Schedule them like any other priority — see time management.

When to get help

Talk to a trusted adult, school counselor or doctor if stress, sadness or worry lasts for weeks or affects sleep, eating, school or relationships. In the US, you can call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) any time, 24/7, if you or someone you know is struggling. Outside the US, contact local emergency services or a national helpline.

This guide is general educational information, not medical advice.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal to feel stressed in high school?

Yes, many students do. If stress feels unmanageable or lasts for weeks, talk to a counselor or trusted adult.

How can parents help with stress?

Listen without immediately problem-solving, keep expectations realistic, protect sleep and downtime, and model balance.
