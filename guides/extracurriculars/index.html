🏅 Extracurricular Activities That Actually Matter

How to choose extracurriculars, what colleges really look for (depth, impact, leadership), how to describe activities on applications, and ideas for every interest.

📅 Updated October 2026✍️ High.School Editorial Team✅ Editorial policy

Colleges don’t have a checklist of “right” activities. They look for depth (sustained involvement), impact (what changed because you were there) and initiative (things you started or grew). Two activities done seriously beat ten done casually.

Types of activities that count

  • Clubs, sports, arts and music
  • Part-time jobs and family responsibilities (caring for siblings counts!)
  • Volunteering and community service
  • Independent projects: a website, a business, research, a YouTube channel
  • Summer programs and online courses

Turn participation into impact

Ask: what problem can I solve here? Organize an event, mentor younger members, raise money, build something. Then measure it — “raised $2,400 for…” is stronger than “helped with fundraising.”

Describing activities on applications

The Common App gives 150 characters per activity description. Lead with action verbs and numbers: “Founded coding club; grew to 35 members; taught weekly Python lessons; built school lunch-menu app.”

Frequently asked questions

How many extracurriculars should I have?

Quality over quantity. Many strong applicants have 3–6 meaningful activities, with deep involvement in 1–2.

Do jobs count as extracurriculars?

Yes. Colleges value work experience — it shows responsibility and time management.
