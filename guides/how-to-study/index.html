🧠 How to Study Effectively: 7 Science-Backed Methods

The study methods research actually supports — retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, elaboration and more — with a weekly plan you can start today.

📅 Updated October 2026✍️ High.School Editorial Team✅ Editorial policy

Most students study the way that feels productive: re-reading notes, highlighting and cramming. Decades of learning-science research show these are among the least effective methods. The techniques below feel harder in the moment — that difficulty is exactly what makes them work.

1. Retrieval practice (test yourself)

Close the book and pull information out of memory: answer practice questions, use flashcards, or write everything you remember on a blank page. Each retrieval strengthens the memory and shows you what you don’t know.

2. Spaced practice

Spread study over days instead of one long session. Reviewing a topic on day 1, day 3, day 7 and day 14 beats the same total time crammed the night before.

3. Interleaving

Mix different problem types in one session (e.g., several kinds of algebra problems instead of 20 of the same). It trains you to choose the right method — exactly what tests require.

4. Elaboration

Ask “how?” and “why?” and connect new ideas to what you already know. Explaining a concept in your own words builds deeper understanding.

5. Concrete examples

Abstract ideas stick when attached to specific examples. For every definition, write one real-world example of your own.

6. Dual coding

Combine words with visuals: diagrams, timelines, flowcharts. Draw a process from memory, then compare with the textbook.

7. Protect sleep and focus

Memory consolidates during sleep, and multitasking with a phone nearby measurably lowers learning. Use the study timer for distraction-free sprints.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours should a high schooler study?

It varies by course load. Many students do 1–3 hours of homework and review on school nights. Focused, active study beats long passive hours.

Is highlighting useless?

Highlighting alone does little. If you highlight, follow up by turning highlights into questions you quiz yourself on.
