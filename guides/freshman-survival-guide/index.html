🎒 Freshman Survival Guide: How to Start High School Strong

Everything new 9th graders need: first-week tips, how grades and GPA work now, getting organized, finding your people, and avoiding the most common freshman mistakes.

📅 Updated October 2026✍️ High.School Editorial Team✅ Editorial policy

Ninth grade is a fresh start — and it counts. Freshman grades are part of the GPA colleges see, and habits you build now will carry you through senior year. Here’s how to start strong.

First week checklist

  • Walk your schedule so you know every room and the fastest routes.
  • Learn each teacher’s grading policy, late-work rules and how to get extra help.
  • Set up your planner and your school’s grade portal.
  • Introduce yourself to your counselor.

Grades work differently now

Every semester grade goes on your permanent transcript. Learn how your GPA is calculated and use the grade calculator to watch each class.

Get organized

One binder or folder per class, one planner for everything, and a 10-minute nightly reset: check the planner, pack the bag, review one set of notes.

Find your people

Try at least two clubs, teams or activities in the first month. It’s the fastest way to make friends and discover interests — and you can drop what doesn’t fit.

Common freshman mistakes

  1. Waiting until a grade is low to ask for help.
  2. Skipping small assignments — they add up.
  3. Staying up late on devices and running on no sleep.
  4. Thinking freshman year doesn’t matter for college.

Frequently asked questions

Does freshman year matter for college?

Yes. Most colleges see all four years of grades, though many value an upward trend. A strong start gives you more options.

How do I make friends in high school?

Join activities, sit with new people at lunch once a week, and say yes to group projects and events.
