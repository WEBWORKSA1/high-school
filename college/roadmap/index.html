Why a roadmap beats cramming senior year

Colleges read your transcript across all four years, and many opportunities — National Merit (11th-grade PSAT), summer programs, leadership roles — have early deadlines. Students who plan ahead take the right courses at the right time and keep senior fall free for applications instead of catching up.

For parents

Your role shifts each year: in 9th grade, help build habits and explore; in 10th–11th, help research options and budget; in 12th, support deadlines and financial aid paperwork. See our Parent Guide and the college cost calculator.

Not planning on a four-year college?

The same roadmap works for community college, apprenticeships, the military or trade school — swap campus visits for program visits and apprenticeship applications. Explore majors & careers.

Frequently asked questions

When should I start preparing for college?

Ninth grade — not with stress, but with good grades, exploring activities and knowing your graduation requirements. Most heavy lifting happens in 11th and 12th grade.

Is it too late to start in 11th grade?

Not at all. Focus on strong junior-year grades, testing if needed, and a well-researched college list. Many students do most of their planning in 11th grade.
