📮 How to Apply to College

A step-by-step college application guide: building your list, Early Decision vs Early Action, Common App, recommendations, deadlines and a submission checklist.

📅 Updated October 2026✍️ High.School Editorial Team✅ Editorial policy

Most US students apply to 6–12 colleges through a platform like the Common App (opens August 1), the Coalition application, or a college’s own portal. A strong application tells one consistent story: your transcript, activities, essays and recommendations should all reinforce who you are and what you care about.

Build a balanced list

  • Reach (2–3): your stats are below the college’s middle 50%, or admit rates are very low.
  • Target (3–5): your GPA and scores fall inside the middle 50%.
  • Likely (2–3): your stats are above the range and you can afford it after aid.

Application rounds

RoundTypical deadlineBinding?

Restrictive Early Action (REA)Nov 1No, but limits other early applications
Early Decision IIJan 1–15Yes
Regular DecisionJan 1–Feb 1No
RollingUntil fullNo

National College Decision Day is May 1, when most colleges ask admitted students to commit.

Application checklist

  1. Personal information and family details
  2. Transcript requested from your counselor
  3. Test scores (if submitting) — official reports or self-reported, per college policy
  4. Activities list (up to 10 on the Common App) — lead with impact and numbers
  5. Personal statement (650 words max on the Common App) — see essay guide
  6. Supplemental essays (“Why us?”, community, intellectual interest)
  7. Two teacher recommendations + counselor recommendation (ask by spring of junior year)
  8. Application fee or fee waiver
  9. FAFSA and CSS Profile for aid

Fee waivers: students who qualify for SAT/ACT fee waivers or free/reduced lunch can usually apply to colleges for free. Ask your counselor.

Frequently asked questions

How many colleges should I apply to?

Most students apply to 6–12. The key is balance: every school on your list should be one you’d be happy to attend and could afford.

Should I apply Early Decision?

Only if a college is your clear first choice and you’ve used its Net Price Calculator to confirm the cost works, since ED is binding.
