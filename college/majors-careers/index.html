🧭 Majors & Careers: Find Your Direction

How to explore majors and careers in high school: interest inventories, career clusters, what to take now, job shadowing, and paths beyond four-year college.

📅 Updated October 2026✍️ High.School Editorial Team✅ Editorial policy

You don’t need to know your career at 15. But exploring early helps you pick courses, activities and summer experiences with purpose — and many students change direction several times. The goal is to test ideas cheaply now so you make better decisions later.

A 4-step exploration method

  1. Inventory: list subjects you enjoy, problems you like solving, and how you like to work (with people, data, things, ideas).
  2. Research: use the US Bureau of Labor Statistics’ Occupational Outlook Handbook for pay, education and growth outlook.
  3. Test: job-shadow, volunteer, take an elective, try a summer program or a part-time job.
  4. Reflect: what energized you? What drained you? Adjust.

Paths beyond a four-year degree

Community college (including transfer pathways), apprenticeships (earn while you learn), military service, certificates and trade schools are all strong routes — many skilled trades offer solid pay and demand. The right path is the one that fits your goals and finances.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to pick a major when I apply to college?

Usually not. Many colleges let you apply undecided. Some competitive programs (engineering, nursing) require you to apply directly.

How do I find job shadowing?

Ask family, teachers and your counselor; contact local businesses, hospitals and offices politely by email; check your school’s CTE program.
