Chemistry explains why matter behaves the way it does — why salt dissolves, why iron rusts, why batteries work. It sits between biology and physics and mixes concepts with math. Most students find the turning point is the mole: once unit conversions feel automatic, the rest of the course opens up.

Course names and order vary by state and school. This is the most common US pattern:

Key topics to master

Atomic structure

Protons, neutrons, electrons, isotopes and electron configuration.

Periodic trends

Atomic radius, electronegativity and ionization energy across periods and down groups.

Bonding

Ionic vs covalent bonds, Lewis structures, molecular shape (VSEPR) and polarity.

The mole & stoichiometry

Avogadro’s number (6.022 × 10²³), molar mass, and using balanced equations to predict amounts.

Reactions

Synthesis, decomposition, single/double replacement, combustion; balancing equations.

Gas laws

Boyle’s, Charles’s and the ideal gas law PV = nRT.

Acids, bases & equilibrium

pH scale, neutralization, and Le Chatelier’s principle.

Thermochemistry

Endothermic vs exothermic reactions and enthalpy.

How to study chemistry effectively

  1. Master dimensional analysis (unit conversions) early — it powers stoichiometry, gas laws and solutions.
  2. Learn the periodic table’s logic, not just its layout; trends let you predict properties you never memorized.
  3. Work problems with units on every number. Units that don’t cancel reveal setup mistakes.
  4. Treat labs as test prep. Write predictions before you start and explain any surprising results.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Changing subscripts instead of coefficients when balancing equations.
  • Forgetting to convert grams to moles before using the mole ratio.
  • Using Celsius instead of Kelvin in gas law calculations.
  • Dropping significant figures or units at the final step.

Practice quiz

✏️ Chemistry quick check

Tap an answer — you’ll see the explanation instantly.

1. What is the molar mass of water (H₂O)? (H = 1, O = 16)

2(1) + 16 = 18 g/mol.

2. Balance: _H₂ + _O₂ → _H₂O

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O gives 4 H and 2 O on each side.

3. A solution with pH 3 is…

pH below 7 is acidic; 7 is neutral; above 7 is basic.

4. Moving left to right across a period, atomic radius generally…

More protons pull the electrons closer, shrinking the radius.

5. In the ideal gas law, temperature must be in…

Gas laws require absolute temperature: K = °C + 273.15.

Frequently asked questions

Is chemistry harder than biology?

Many students find it harder because it uses algebra and abstract models. Strong algebra skills and early practice with unit conversions make a big difference.

Do I need to memorize the periodic table?

Usually no — you’re given one on tests. Know the common elements, group names and trends instead.

How do I get better at stoichiometry?

Follow the same path every time: grams → moles → mole ratio → moles → grams. Practice 10 problems in a row until the path is automatic.
