Biology is the study of living things, from a single molecule of DNA to entire ecosystems. It is usually the first lab science students take in high school, and it is vocabulary-heavy: a typical course introduces hundreds of new terms. The good news is that biology rewards understanding systems — once you see how structure relates to function, the vocabulary starts to organize itself.

Key topics to master

Biochemistry

Carbohydrates, lipids, proteins and nucleic acids; enzymes as biological catalysts.

Cells

Prokaryotic vs eukaryotic cells, organelles, the cell membrane and transport (diffusion, osmosis, active transport).

Energy

Photosynthesis stores energy in glucose; cellular respiration releases it as ATP.

Genetics

DNA structure, replication, transcription and translation, Mendelian inheritance and Punnett squares.

Evolution

Natural selection, adaptation, evidence from fossils and DNA, and speciation.

Ecology

Food webs, energy pyramids, nutrient cycles and population dynamics.

Human body systems

How the circulatory, respiratory, nervous, immune and other systems maintain homeostasis.

How to study biology effectively

  1. Draw processes. Sketch mitosis, protein synthesis or the carbon cycle from memory, then check against your notes.
  2. Use flashcards for vocabulary — but write the definition in your own words and add a real example.
  3. Connect structure to function: ask “why is it shaped like that?” for every organelle and organ.
  4. Practice data questions. Modern exams emphasize reading graphs and designing experiments, not just recall.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Mixing up mitosis (2 identical cells) and meiosis (4 genetically different gametes).
  • Saying plants do photosynthesis instead of respiration — they do both.
  • Confusing genotype (alleles) with phenotype (observable trait).
  • Thinking individuals evolve — populations evolve over generations.

Practice quiz

✏️ Biology quick check

Tap an answer — you’ll see the explanation instantly.

1. Which organelle produces most of a cell’s ATP?

Mitochondria carry out cellular respiration, producing ATP.

2. In DNA, adenine pairs with…

A pairs with T (in RNA, A pairs with U); C pairs with G.

3. Two heterozygous parents (Aa × Aa) — what fraction of offspring is expected to show the recessive trait?

Only aa shows the recessive trait: 1 of 4 Punnett-square boxes.

4. Water moving across a membrane from low to high solute concentration is called…

Osmosis is the diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane.

5. About how much energy passes from one trophic level to the next?

The 10% rule: most energy is lost as heat at each level.

Frequently asked questions

Is biology hard in high school?

It is less math-heavy than chemistry or physics but requires steady memorization. Short daily review with flashcards beats cramming.

Should I take AP Biology?

If you enjoyed biology and plan on health, life-science or pre-med paths, AP Biology is valuable. It has a heavy workload, so balance it with your other courses.

What is the best way to memorize biology terms?

Spaced repetition: review cards at increasing intervals, say definitions aloud in your own words, and connect each term to a diagram.
