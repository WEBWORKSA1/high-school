Math in high school is a ladder: every rung depends on the one below it. Students who struggle in Algebra 2 or Precalculus are usually not “bad at math” — they’re missing a specific skill from two years earlier, like manipulating fractions or solving for a variable. The fastest way up is to find that gap and fix it.

This guide maps the typical course sequence, the topics that show up on every test (and on the SAT and ACT), and a study method that works for problem-based subjects.

Typical Math course sequence

Course names and order vary by state and school. This is the most common US pattern:

GradeTypical course(s)
9Algebra 1 (or Geometry if accelerated)
10Geometry (or Algebra 2)
11Algebra 2 / Precalculus
12Precalculus, AP Calculus AB/BC, AP Statistics or a math elective

Key topics to master

Linear equations & inequalities

Solving for a variable, slope-intercept form y = mx + b, systems of equations. The single most-tested skill on the SAT Math section.

Functions

Domain and range, function notation, transformations (shifts, stretches, reflections). Think of a function as a machine: one input, exactly one output.

Quadratics & polynomials

Factoring, the quadratic formula, vertex form, and reading roots from a graph.

Exponents & logarithms

Exponential growth/decay (compound interest, populations) and logs as the inverse operation.

Geometry & proof

Congruence, similarity, the Pythagorean theorem, circles, area and volume — plus writing logical two-column proofs.

Trigonometry

SOH-CAH-TOA, the unit circle, radians, and trig identities.

Calculus

Limits, derivatives (rate of change) and integrals (accumulated change).

Statistics & probability

Mean/median, standard deviation, sampling, and reading data displays — increasingly important on modern tests.

How to study math effectively

  1. Do problems, not pages. Reading a worked example feels productive but builds little skill. Cover the solution and attempt it first.
  2. Keep an error log. For each missed problem write: what you did, the correct step, and the rule. Review the log weekly.
  3. Mix problem types (interleaving). Real tests don’t tell you which chapter a problem is from.
  4. Show every step on homework so you — or a tutor — can spot exactly where a mistake begins.
  5. Use free graphing tools such as Desmos to check answers visually; the digital SAT includes a built-in Desmos calculator.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Distributing a negative sign incorrectly: −(x − 3) = −x + 3, not −x − 3.
  • Squaring a sum: (a + b)² ≠ a² + b² — it’s a² + 2ab + b².
  • Forgetting to flip an inequality when multiplying or dividing by a negative.
  • Calculator in degree mode when the problem uses radians (or vice-versa).

✏️ Math quick check

Tap an answer — you’ll see the explanation instantly.

1. Solve: 3x − 7 = 11

Add 7 to both sides (3x = 18), then divide by 3: x = 6.

2. What is the slope of the line through (2, 3) and (6, 11)?

Slope = (11 − 3) / (6 − 2) = 8 / 4 = 2.

3. Which is equivalent to (x + 3)²?

(x + 3)(x + 3) = x² + 3x + 3x + 9 = x² + 6x + 9.

4. A right triangle has legs 6 and 8. The hypotenuse is…

6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100, and √100 = 10.

5. If f(x) = 2x² − 1, what is f(−3)?

f(−3) = 2(9) − 1 = 17. Squaring a negative gives a positive.

Frequently asked questions

What order should I take high school math classes in?

Most US schools follow Algebra 1 → Geometry → Algebra 2 → Precalculus → Calculus or Statistics. Some states use an integrated Math 1-2-3 sequence that mixes these topics each year.

Do I need calculus for college?

Not for admission everywhere, but selective colleges and STEM majors strongly prefer four years of math including precalculus or calculus. Check the specific college’s recommended preparation.

How can I get better at math fast?

Find the earliest skill gap (often fractions, negatives or solving for a variable), drill it for a week with daily mixed practice, and keep an error log. A tutor speeds this up by diagnosing the gap quickly.
