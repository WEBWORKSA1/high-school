Physics describes how the universe moves, from a thrown ball to orbiting planets. It is the most math-applied science in high school, but it’s really about a handful of big ideas — forces, energy and conservation laws — applied over and over. Learn those deeply and most problems become variations on a theme.

Typical Physics course sequence

Course names and order vary by state and school. This is the most common US pattern:

GradeTypical course(s)
9 (Physics First schools) or 11–12Physics / Conceptual Physics
11–12AP Physics 1 & 2 (algebra-based), AP Physics C (calculus-based), IB Physics

Key topics to master

Kinematics

Displacement, velocity, acceleration and the four motion equations.

Newton’s laws

Inertia, F = ma, action–reaction, and free-body diagrams.

Energy & work

Kinetic and potential energy, power, and conservation of energy.

Momentum

Impulse, collisions and conservation of momentum.

Circular motion & gravity

Centripetal force and universal gravitation.

Electricity & circuits

Charge, current, voltage, resistance and Ohm’s law V = IR.

Waves, sound & light

Frequency, wavelength, the wave equation v = fλ, reflection and refraction.

How to study physics effectively

  1. Always draw a diagram — free-body diagrams solve half of all force problems.
  2. List knowns and unknowns with units before choosing an equation.
  3. Check with intuition: does a heavier object really fall faster? (In a vacuum, no.)
  4. Explain concepts out loud; AP Physics 1 is heavily conceptual and includes written explanations.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Treating mass and weight as the same thing (weight = mg).
  • Ignoring direction — velocity and force are vectors.
  • Mixing units (cm with m, minutes with seconds).
  • Assuming an object needs a force to keep moving at constant velocity.

Practice quiz

✏️ Physics quick check

Tap an answer — you’ll see the explanation instantly.

1. A 2 kg object accelerates at 3 m/s². The net force is…

F = ma = 2 × 3 = 6 N.

2. Which quantity is a vector?

Velocity has magnitude and direction.

3. If voltage is 12 V and resistance is 4 Ω, current is…

I = V/R = 12/4 = 3 A.

4. A ball is dropped. Ignoring air, its acceleration is about…

Free-fall acceleration near Earth’s surface is about 9.8 m/s² downward.

5. A wave has frequency 5 Hz and wavelength 2 m. Its speed is…

v = fλ = 5 × 2 = 10 m/s.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need calculus for high school physics?

No. Standard and AP Physics 1/2 use algebra and trigonometry. Only AP Physics C uses calculus.

What’s the difference between AP Physics 1 and AP Physics C?

AP Physics 1 is algebra-based and conceptual; AP Physics C (Mechanics; Electricity & Magnetism) is calculus-based and aimed at future engineers and physicists.

How do I study for a physics test?

Redo homework problems without notes, explain each step, and practice problems that combine two concepts (e.g., energy and momentum).
