Psychology is the scientific study of behavior and mental processes. It’s one of the most popular high school electives and AP exams, and it’s immediately useful: learning how memory works will improve how you study for every other class.

Typical Psychology course sequence

Course names and order vary by state and school. This is the most common US pattern:

GradeTypical course(s)
10–12Psychology elective or AP Psychology
11–12Sociology elective; IB Psychology where offered

Key topics to master

Research methods

Experiments vs correlational studies, variables, sampling, and ethics.

Biological bases

Neurons, neurotransmitters, brain regions and the nervous system.

Sensation & perception

How senses detect stimuli and the brain interprets them.

Learning

Classical conditioning, operant conditioning and observational learning.

Memory & cognition

Encoding, storage, retrieval, and why we forget.

Development

Physical, cognitive and social development across the lifespan.

Social psychology

Conformity, obedience, attitudes and group behavior.

How to study psychology effectively

  1. Apply each concept to your own life — the self-reference effect makes it stick.
  2. Learn the classic studies by their question, method, result and criticism.
  3. Use the concepts to study better: retrieval practice and spacing are psychology findings.
  4. Practice applying terms to scenarios — that’s how AP Psych free-response questions work.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Thinking correlation proves causation.
  • Mixing up negative reinforcement (removing something unpleasant) with punishment.
  • Confusing the independent variable (what you change) with the dependent variable (what you measure).

Practice quiz

✏️ Psychology quick check

Tap an answer — you’ll see the explanation instantly.

1. A study finds ice cream sales and sunburns rise together. This shows…

Both are caused by a third variable: hot, sunny weather.

2. Taking away chores when a student earns good grades is…

Removing something unpleasant to increase a behavior = negative reinforcement.

3. Pavlov’s dogs salivating at a bell demonstrates…

A neutral stimulus (bell) became associated with food.

4. In an experiment, the variable the researcher manipulates is the…

The independent variable is changed; the dependent variable is measured.

5. Testing yourself instead of re-reading notes is called…

Retrieval practice strengthens memory more than re-reading.

Frequently asked questions

Is AP Psychology hard?

It is considered one of the more accessible AP exams, but there’s a lot of vocabulary. Applying terms to real examples is the key skill.

Is psychology a science?

Yes — psychology uses the scientific method, experiments and statistics to test ideas about behavior and the mind.

What careers use psychology?

Counseling, clinical psychology, social work, marketing, UX research, human resources, education and medicine all draw on psychology.
