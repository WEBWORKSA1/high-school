How GPA is calculated

Each letter grade converts to grade points on a 4.0 scale (A = 4.0, B = 3.0, C = 2.0, D = 1.0, F = 0, with plus/minus steps). Multiply each course’s points by its credits, add them up, and divide by total credits. That’s your unweighted GPA.

Weighted GPA adds a bonus for harder courses — commonly +0.5 for Honors and +1.0 for AP, IB or dual-enrollment — so an A in AP Biology counts as 5.0. Schools differ: some cap weighting, some use 4.33 for A+, and some don’t weight at all. Colleges often recalculate GPA their own way, so report what your transcript shows.

What is a good high school GPA?

The answer depends on your goals. A 3.0 unweighted meets the minimum for many state universities and scholarships; 3.5+ is competitive for many selective colleges; the most selective colleges typically see 3.9+ unweighted with a rigorous schedule. Upward trends matter too — admissions readers notice improvement.

How to raise your GPA

  • Focus on the class with the most credits and the most room to improve — it moves the average most.
  • Use the final grade calculator to see what you need on upcoming exams.
  • Ask about retake, test-correction or extra-credit policies early in the term.
  • Fix study habits, not just effort: see how to study effectively.

Frequently asked questions

What’s the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA?

Unweighted GPA treats every class the same on a 4.0 scale. Weighted GPA adds points for Honors, AP, IB or dual-enrollment classes, so it can exceed 4.0.

Do colleges look at weighted or unweighted GPA?

Both, and many recalculate using only core academic classes. They also look at course rigor — the difficulty of your schedule relative to what your school offers.
