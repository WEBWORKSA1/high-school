🏅 PSAT/NMSQT & National Merit Guide

How the PSAT/NMSQT works, how it’s scored (320–1520), the National Merit Selection Index, and how juniors qualify for recognition and scholarships.

📅 Updated October 2026✍️ High.School Editorial Team✅ Editorial policy

The PSAT/NMSQT is a practice version of the SAT, usually taken in October. When taken in 11th grade, it is also the qualifying test for the National Merit Scholarship Program. It uses the same digital, adaptive format as the SAT.

Scoring

Total scores range from 320 to 1520 (Reading & Writing 160–760, Math 160–760). National Merit uses a separate Selection Index (48–228), which double-weights Reading & Writing: Selection Index = (2 × RW + Math) ÷ 10.

National Merit recognition

  • Roughly the top 50,000 Selection Index scorers receive recognition.
  • About 16,000 are named Semifinalists, based on state-specific cutoffs.
  • Semifinalists who complete an application (grades, recommendation, essay, confirming SAT/ACT score) can become Finalists, and some receive scholarships.

How to prepare

Because the PSAT mirrors the SAT, SAT prep is PSAT prep. Take a full practice test in the Bluebook app in summer before junior year, focus on Reading & Writing (it counts double for National Merit), and review your score report to plan SAT prep.

Frequently asked questions

Does the PSAT count for college admissions?

No. Colleges don’t see PSAT scores. It matters for National Merit (junior year) and as SAT practice.

Can 10th graders take the PSAT?

Yes, many do for practice, but only the junior-year score counts for National Merit.
