🧾 The ACT: Complete Guide

The enhanced ACT explained — shorter test, optional science, composite scoring, timing per question, what’s a good score and how to prepare.

📅 Updated October 2026✍️ High.School Editorial Team✅ Editorial policy

The ACT is the other major US college admission test. In 2025 ACT introduced the enhanced ACT: a shorter test with fewer questions, more time per question, and the Science section made optional. Colleges accept the ACT and SAT equally.

Enhanced ACT format

SectionQuestionsTimeNotes
English5035 minGrammar, punctuation, rhetorical skills
Math4550 minCalculator allowed; 4 answer choices
Reading3640 minPassages from literature, social science, humanities, natural science
Science (optional)4040 minData interpretation, experiments, conflicting viewpoints
Writing (optional)1 essay40 minArgumentative essay

The composite score (1–36) is the average of English, Math and Reading. If you take Science, you also receive a Science score and a STEM score. Check whether the colleges and scholarship programs on your list want the Science section.

What is a good ACT score?

The national average composite is around 19–20. Roughly: 24+ is solid for many state universities, 28+ is strong for selective colleges, and 33+ is in range for the most selective. Compare with SAT scores using our converter.

Strategy

  • Pacing first. Even on the enhanced test, the ACT rewards speed. Practice with a timer from day one.
  • English is the fastest section to improve. A small set of grammar rules (commas, semicolons, apostrophes, subject–verb agreement, concision) covers most questions.
  • Math: memorize core formulas — the ACT does not provide a formula sheet.
  • Reading: try reading the passage briskly for structure, then hunt for evidence for each answer.
  • Science: it is mostly graph and table reading — you rarely need outside science knowledge.

Frequently asked questions

Is ACT Science still required?

On the enhanced ACT, Science is optional and not included in the composite score. Some colleges or programs may still want it — check their requirements.

Is the ACT easier than the SAT?

Neither is objectively easier. The ACT is faster-paced and has no built-in graphing calculator; the SAT is adaptive and has more algebra emphasis. Take a practice test of each.

Do colleges superscore the ACT?

Many do. ACT also reports a superscore if you have taken the test more than once.
