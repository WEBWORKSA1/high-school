⚔️ SAT vs ACT: Which Should You Take?

Side-by-side comparison of the digital SAT and enhanced ACT — timing, content, calculators, scoring — plus a 1-minute quiz to find your better test.

📅 Updated October 2026✍️ High.School Editorial Team✅ Editorial policy

Every US college that accepts test scores accepts both the SAT and the ACT equally. So the question isn’t which test colleges prefer — it’s which test you will score higher on. Most students score similarly on both, but about one in three has a meaningful edge on one test.

⚔️ 1-minute SAT vs ACT quiz

Pick the statement that sounds most like you.

1. Under time pressure, I…
2. In math, I’m stronger at…
3. For calculators, I want…
4. On reading, I prefer…
5. Science-style data questions…
6. Test format I’d enjoy…

Side-by-side comparison

Digital SATEnhanced ACT
Length2 h 14 min~2 h core (+40 min optional Science)
FormatDigital, adaptive by modulePaper or online, linear
SectionsReading & Writing; MathEnglish; Math; Reading; optional Science & Writing
CalculatorAll math; Desmos built inAll math; bring an approved calculator
Reading styleShort passages, 1 question eachLonger passages, multiple questions
Scoring400–16001–36 composite

How to decide

  1. Take one full official practice test of each, under timed conditions.
  2. Convert scores with our SAT ↔ ACT converter.
  3. Pick the test with the higher converted score — and the one you felt better on — then commit to it.

Frequently asked questions

Do colleges prefer the SAT or ACT?

No. Colleges that accept test scores treat them equally.

Should I take both?

Usually it’s better to choose one and prepare deeply. Take a practice test of each first, then commit.
